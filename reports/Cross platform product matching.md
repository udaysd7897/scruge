# Extract attributes, veto mismatches, then pick the match

To match an exact product variant across Amazon India, Flipkart, Croma and Myntra, extract the attributes that define the variant from both listings, apply them as hard equality rules, and use similarity scores and LLMs only to rank or break ties among the candidates that survive. The research behind this rule is consistent. Every learned matcher has trouble with near-identical variants. On WDC Products, the benchmark built around such "corner cases", fine-tuned transformers score only **78–85 F1** and zero-shot GPT-4 scores **89.6** ([Peeters et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)). GPT-4's own errors come mostly from **overweighting the attributes two listings share** and from **misreading small model-number differences** ([Peeters et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)). Fuzzy string scores give a perfect 100 whenever one title's words are a subset of the other's ([RapidFuzz](https://rapidfuzz.github.io/RapidFuzz/Usage/fuzz.html)). The standard entity-matching libraries (Ditto, Dedupe, Splink, Magellan) solve problems this system does not have: blocking over large tables, and training on labelled pairs. The Indian open-source price comparators mostly take the first search result or apply a TF-IDF or fuzzy threshold. The production pattern to copy is the precision-first cascade used by Shopify and a 2026 product-linking paper: cheap scoring, a calibrated auto-accept, and escalation or abstention for the rest ([Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/catalog-clustering-part-ii); [arXiv 2608.25037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25037)). For a take-home with 10–20 candidates per platform, that means seven steps: query each platform's search with a ladder of queries; extract brand, model family and qualifiers, RAM, storage, screen size and colour with regex plus an LLM; veto any conflict; rank the survivors; accept only a single clear winner; read stock status from that exact variant's structured data; and keep "Not listed" separate from "check failed". Expect strong coverage on phones and TVs. On apparel, even commercial vendors report match rates of only **40–60%** ([DataWeave](https://dataweave.com/blog/from-mess-to-match-solving-product-identity-at-retail-scale)).

## Identifiers are decisive when present, and Flipkart and Myntra rarely provide them

The strongest signal of identity is a shared GTIN/EAN. GS1 requires a separate GTIN for every colour, every size and every colour×size combination, so a T-shirt sold in three colours needs three GTINs ([GS1](https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734083-how-many-gs1-gtins-do-i-need-when-i-have-a-product-with-many-sizes-and-colours-)). An exact EAN match is therefore a variant-level match. Google Shopping relies on this. It asks merchants for distinct identifiers per colour and size variant, and warns that without a GTIN "accurate matching to products can't be assured" ([Google Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324461)). In practice, though, identifiers are patchy. Shopify, which clusters billions of listings, says barcodes "are not consistently available or reliable" and relies mostly on learned text and image models ([Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/catalog-clustering-part-ii)). DataWeave makes the same point: retailers omit identifiers, swap in internal SKUs, or list multipacks under unit codes ([DataWeave](https://dataweave.com/blog/from-mess-to-match-solving-product-identity-at-retail-scale)). The take-home's data sources show the same split. Croma, via Apify, exposes **EAN and Model Number**. Amazon sometimes lists them in its technical-details table. Flipkart's JSON-LD `sku` is probably Flipkart's internal ID rather than a manufacturer part number; this is an inference, so verify it. Myntra's search JSON has no model code at all. Identifier matching will mostly help for Amazon↔Croma electronics, and attribute matching has to do the work everywhere else. Treat an EAN match, after validating the check digit, as near-certain. Treat an EAN mismatch as a strong but not absolute negative, because sellers mis-key codes. A missing EAN is neutral.

Manufacturer model codes are the next-best key, but they have to be parsed rather than compared as strings. In a Samsung code, the base (SM-S921) identifies the device, and the suffix letter marks the regional variant: B for Europe, U for the USA, 0 for China ([iFixit](https://ifixit.com/Device/Samsung_Galaxy_S24)). Samsung India's own SKUs, such as sm-m558blgcins, also encode colour and storage ([Samsung IN](https://www.samsung.com/in/smartphones/galaxy-m/galaxy-m55s-green-256gb-sm-m558blgcins/)). Sony TV codes carry the screen size: the 55 in KD-55X80L means 55 inches ([Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/technology/tvs-home-cinema/sony-tv-model-numbers-explained)). Nike style codes split into a model prefix and a colourway suffix, so DA2142-146 (White) and DA2142-046 (Black) are the same shoe in different colours ([House of Heat](https://houseofheat.co/nike/nike-blazer-mid-77-color-code-white-da2142-146-black-da2142-046)). A US patent on automated matching scores model numbers the same way: a shared code scores 100, and codes that are present on both sides but differ score 0 ([USPTO 11367116](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11367116)). The practical rule is to normalize codes (uppercase, strip separators) and parse them into base and suffix. When both sides have a code, a base mismatch is a hard negative. Be careful with suffixes: LG's 55UQ8020PSB and 55UQ8040PSB are different models, so do not strip trailing characters blindly.

When there is no identifier, the system has to rebuild the variant from text, and Indian listings make that hard. Flipkart phone titles follow "Brand Model (Colour, Storage)" and put RAM in the highlights ("8 GB RAM | 256 GB ROM"). Croma URLs follow "brand-model-5g-8gb-ram-256gb-colour" ([Flipkart POCO M7 Pro](https://flipkart.com/poco-m7-pro-5g-olive-twilight-256-gb/p/itm1d88319131b56); [Croma A56](https://www.croma.com/samsung-galaxy-a56-5g-8gb-ram-256gb-awesome-lightgray-/p/314441)). For TVs, Flipkart lists one nominal 55-inch size as "139 cm", "139.7 cm" or "138.71 cm" depending on the listing. One Flipkart URL slug says 65 inch and resolves to a 55-inch item, so the page has to be parsed rather than the URL ([Flipkart LG UQ8020](https://flipkart.com/lg-uq8020-139-cm-55-inch-ultra-hd-4k-led-smart-webos-tv-2022/p/itmb16e05765d7b2); [Flipkart LG slug anomaly](https://www.flipkart.com/lg-164-cm-65-inch-ultra-hd-4k-led-smart-webos-tv/p/itm14880889af054)). Myntra titles describe the colour in marketing language ("Nike Men Black & Blue Colourblocked Court Vision LO Leather Sneakers") rather than using Nike's official colourway or style code ([PriceHistory.app](https://pricehistoryapp.com/product/nike-men-black-blue-colourblocked-court-vision-lo-leather-sneakers)).

The research supports a hybrid extractor. On the WDC-PAVE benchmark, GPT-4 with JSON-schema output and examples reaches **90.5 F1** on extraction and **96.1 F1** on normalization alone. The best pre-LLM extractor (AVEQA) reaches 80.8. Unit conversion is the hardest operation ([Brinkmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02130v4)). Use deterministic regexes for the fixed fields: GB, TB, inches and cm, and model codes. Then add an LLM pass through Instructor or Outlines with nullable fields and an `evidence_span` that quotes the source text, and reject any value whose span is not in the input ([Instructor](https://github.com/567-labs/instructor); [Outlines](https://github.com/dottxt-ai/outlines)). The regexes need guards for traps specific to India. "8GB RAM + 8GB Virtual RAM" means 8 GB of RAM, not 16. Model-family qualifiers (Plus, Pro, Max, Ultra, FE, Lite, 5G) are part of the model's identity, so S24 and S24 FE are different products. TV centimetres should be converted with cm/2.54 and snapped to the nearest standard size, so that "164 cm (65)" becomes 65 rather than 64.6.

## Similarity scores rank candidates but cannot certify a variant

The benchmark literature agrees on one point. Every surface-similarity method does worst exactly where this task is hardest: two listings that agree on almost everything and differ on one number. WDC Products was built to measure this. It contains 2,162 products from 3,259 e-shops, with test sets of up to **80% corner cases**, and its label noise is about 4%, which caps realistic F1 near 96 ([WDC Products](https://webdatacommons.org/largescaleproductcorpus/wdc-products/)). Raising the corner-case share from 20% to 80% costs every model 7–10 F1. Products the models never saw in training cost more: Ditto falls from 79.1 on seen products to 68.4 on unseen ones, and the contrastive R-SupCon model falls from 82.2 to 53.3, because it memorizes product clusters ([WDC Products](https://webdatacommons.org/largescaleproductcorpus/wdc-products/)). Cross-platform matching is almost always an unseen-product problem. That favours methods that generalize over methods trained on a fixed catalogue.

LLMs currently lead on unseen products. The table below gives zero-shot F1 with the best prompt, from the Mannheim group's EDBT 2025 study.

| Model | WDC Products (80% corner cases) | Abt-Buy | Walmart-Amazon | Amazon-Google |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-4 (zero-shot) | 89.61 | 95.78 | 89.67 | 76.38 |
| GPT-4o (zero-shot) | 87.64 | 93.95 | 86.65 | 73.56 |
| GPT-4o-mini (zero-shot) | 81.15 | 91.93 | 86.58 | 72.18 |
| Llama-3.1-70B (zero-shot) | 83.67 | 89.84 | 84.85 | 73.99 |
| Ditto (fine-tuned) | 84.90 | 91.31 | 86.39 | 80.07 |
| RoBERTa (fine-tuned) | 77.53 | 91.21 | 87.02 | 79.27 |

Source: [Peeters, Steiner, Bizer, arXiv 2310.11244v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)

The same paper adds caveats that matter for design. No single prompt is best for every model. GPT-4 is the least sensitive to prompt wording, while smaller models vary by 6–18 F1 across prompts. In-context examples helped GPT-4o but hurt GPT-4o-mini, which dropped from 85.5 to 76.6 mean F1. Fine-tuning GPT-4o-mini lifted it to **88.9** on WDC Products at very low inference cost: roughly **$0.06 per 1,000 pairs** zero-shot at August-2024 prices ([arXiv 2310.11244v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)). The error analysis matters most here. Of GPT-4's 26 false positives on Walmart-Amazon, **23 came from "overemphasis on matching attributes"**, and 9 of its 15 false negatives came from slight model-number differences ([arXiv 2310.11244v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)). An LLM asked "are these the same?" fails in exactly the direction a high-precision system cannot tolerate. It sees "Samsung Galaxy S24 8GB" on both sides and overlooks 128 GB versus 256 GB. No published evaluation covers 2025–26 frontier models or Indian marketplace listings, so these figures set a direction rather than a ceiling.

Classical similarity is worse at deciding but still useful for ranking. RapidFuzz's `token_set_ratio` returns 100 whenever one string is a subset of the other ([RapidFuzz](https://rapidfuzz.github.io/RapidFuzz/Usage/fuzz.html)). A family-level title on Flipkart therefore scores perfectly against a fully specified Amazon variant title. Pretrained transformers handle numbers poorly. Subword tokenizers split codes into fragments, and the "number insensitiveness" of these models motivated numeric-aware matchers such as JointMatcher ([arXiv 2112.08327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.08327); [JointMatcher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0950705122005044)). Ditto's main contribution was to highlight numbers and IDs so the model would attend to them ([arXiv 2004.00584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00584)). Walmart's engineers made the same point from the data side. Random negatives are useless for training, because real mismatches are near-identical except for one attribute such as storage, colour or condition ([Walmart Labs](https://medium.com/walmartlabs/product-matching-in-ecommerce-4f19b6aebaca)). Shopify moved from embedding distance to a joint cross-encoder for this reason, since it matters "when most of the text agrees but one detail changes the identity" ([Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/catalog-clustering-part-ii)).

Images follow the same pattern. Multimodal fusion beats text alone in Amazon's MAPS work, in Leipzig's work on Zalando data, and in a Turkish marketplace that reached 0.90 macro-F1 ([MAPS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com:443/content/WACV2022/papers/Das_MAPS_Multimodal_Attention_for_Product_Similarity_WACV_2022_paper.pdf); [CEUR-WS](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3710/paper14.pdf); [arXiv 2509.15858](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15858v2)). But a 128 GB phone and a 256 GB phone share the same photos. A perceptual-hash patent notes that hashing "may not be suitable" where variants exist ([USPTO 12153623](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12153623)). Images belong only in the apparel and sneaker tie-break. Myntra itself treats colour variants as a distinct visual-representation problem ([Myntra, AAAI 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02910)).

## Open-source projects solve blocking and training, which this system does not need

The classic entity-matching toolkits fall into two groups, and neither fits a real-time matcher that compares one product against 15 candidates. The first group needs labelled pairs: Ditto, DeepMatcher, Magellan's ML matchers, Dedupe and Zingg (Dedupe and Zingg through interactive active learning). The second group is unsupervised but built for linking structured tables at scale: Splink's Fellegi-Sunter model, pyJedAI's blocking and embedding workflows, and recordlinkage's ECM classifier ([Ditto](https://github.com/megagonlabs/ditto); [Dedupe](https://github.com/dedupeio/dedupe); [Splink](https://github.com/moj-analytical-services/splink); [pyJedAI](https://github.com/AI-team-UoA/pyJedAI); [Zingg](https://github.com/zinggAI/zingg)). Blocking, their main engineering contribution, is redundant here, because each retailer's search engine already returns the candidates. Zingg's AGPL-3.0 license is a further problem for a hosted app. Most of the research matchers (HierGAT, Sudowoodo, PromptEM, Unicorn) are paper code that has not been updated since 2022–23 and has no PyPI release. AnyMatch reaches within **4.4%** of GPT-4's average F1 at about **3,899× lower cost**, but only after training on nine public benchmarks ([arXiv 2409.04073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04073)).

Two projects match the shape of this problem. The Mannheim group's MatchGPT repo holds the prompts behind the LLM results above ([MatchGPT](https://github.com/wbsg-uni-mannheim/MatchGPT)). ComEM (COLING 2025) studies a "select" strategy, in which an LLM picks the matching record from a list of candidates, and combines a cheap filter with a stronger model ([ComEM](https://github.com/tshu-w/ComEM); [arXiv 2405.16884](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16884v3)). One anchor product against a list of candidates is exactly the take-home's setup. A single listwise call can replace about 20 pairwise calls. The cost is position bias and a longer context, so shuffle candidate order and require the model to cite attributes.

The Shopee Price Match Guarantee competition on Kaggle (2,426 teams) is the most-studied open product-matching contest. Its top solutions used ArcFace-trained image models (NFNet, EfficientNet) and text models (BERT, XLM, TF-IDF), ran a kNN search per modality, and fed similarity features to a second-stage LightGBM or CatBoost model with graph post-processing ([Shopee 2nd-place summary](https://speakerdeck.com/lyakaap/shopee-2nd-place-solutiontoshang-wei-jie-fa-matome)). That approach needs a large corpus of labelled product groups. It also targets "same or similar product" recall, not exact-variant precision. Two of its lessons do transfer. First, numbers in titles are variant markers: the 2020 Shopee data-science winner handled them explicitly ([Medium](https://budiryan.medium.com/first-place-solution-for-shopee-national-data-science-challenge-2020-b12211242f22)). Second, a second-stage scorer over several features beats a hand-tuned threshold for each modality.

The Indian open-source landscape is student-grade. Most Amazon–Flipkart comparators simply scrape the top search result ([priceChamp](https://github.com/2k4sm/priceChamp); [Amazon-Flipkart-Price-Comparison-Engine](https://github.com/sushantPatrikar/Amazon-Flipkart-Price-Comparison-Engine)). ElectroFind extracts brand, storage, colour and model with regex, then accepts a match at **TF-IDF cosine ≥ 0.85** and claims 94% accuracy on an undescribed test set ([ElectroFind](https://electro-find.vercel.app/docs)). A cosine threshold like that will accept a 128 GB phone as a match for its 256 GB sibling, because the titles differ by one token. The best open reference for exact variants is Kifayati, which covers Amazon, Flipkart, Croma, Myntra and JioMart. Its rules require the brand to be present, every number in the query to appear in the candidate, tier modifiers to be equal, and at least 35% token overlap ([Kifayati](https://github.com/ravadakaran/Kifayati)). Commercial vendors take the same approach. Actowiz matches electronics across Amazon, Flipkart and Croma "by model number and product identifiers" ([Actowiz](https://www.actowizsolutions.com/electronics-price-parity-same-sku.php)). The supporting libraries worth adopting are small: RapidFuzz for ranking ([RapidFuzz](https://github.com/rapidfuzz/RapidFuzz)), Instructor or Outlines for schema-bound extraction, and colornamer's CIEDE2000 colour-family hierarchy as a seed for colour normalization ([colornamer](https://github.com/stitchfix/colornamer)).

## Production systems use precision-first cascades and abstain freely

Shopify's catalogue clustering runs in tiers. An approximate-nearest-neighbour search over embeddings proposes candidates. A GBDT scores cheap features (embedding distance, title and vendor similarity, price ratios). A fine-tuned multilingual-E5 cross-encoder handles harder pairs, and a multimodal reranker sees images only for selected comparisons. Merge thresholds are tuned **for precision first**, because a wrong merge costs more than a missed one ([Shopify](https://shopify.engineering/catalog-clustering-part-ii)). The August 2026 "Retrieve, Match, Escalate" paper adds calibration. A lightweight cross-encoder, distilled from labels on which two vision-language models agreed, auto-accepts links at **98% calibrated precision**. An agentic open-weight VLM, which inspects images and runs web searches, handles the ambiguous remainder, and this escalation lifts coverage from **68% to 77%** ([arXiv 2608.25037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25037)). Both systems reuse their expensive judgments as training data and check LLM labels against human labels, because judge errors propagate.

Price-intelligence vendors describe the same structure, with a human review step. DataWeave auto-verifies or auto-rejects about **90% of pairs** and sends the rest to specialist reviewers, whose decisions become training labels (more than 40M verified pairs). It separates "exact", "similar" and "variant" matches, and warns that treating them as one problem yields "confidently wrong results" ([DataWeave](https://dataweave.com/blog/from-mess-to-match-solving-product-identity-at-retail-scale)). Intelligence Node's "99% accuracy" turns out to mean about one wrong exact match per 100 SKUs, which is a precision figure; recall goes unreported ([Intelligence Node](https://intelligencenode.com/solutions/product-matching)). An unnamed large Indian retailer's pricing tool applies brand, category and price pre-filters before vector search and thresholding ([Zilliz](https://zilliz.com/customers/indian-online-retailer)). The most candid figure in the whole literature comes from the same DataWeave post: apparel match rates of **40–60% versus about 95% in electronics** (vendor-reported). Coverage on T-shirts, polos and jerseys will be low no matter how good the matcher is. That is a property of the domain, not a bug.

Indian comparators such as Smartprix, MySmartPrice and 91mobiles show the target data model in their URLs. They keep canonical pages keyed on model plus RAM and storage, such as "samsung-galaxy-a55-5g-8gb-ram-256gb", and attach Amazon, Flipkart and Croma offers to each page ([Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/mobiles/samsung-galaxy-a55-5g-8gb-ram-256gb-ppd10l49lwem); [MySmartPrice](https://www.mysmartprice.com/mobile/samsung-galaxy-a17-256gb-8gb-ram-msp620220)). Fashion trackers such as PriceHistory.app track each retailer listing separately rather than canonicalizing across stores. That suggests even established players have not solved cross-store apparel matching ([PriceHistory.app](https://pricehistoryapp.com/product/nike-men-black-blue-colourblocked-court-vision-lo-leather-sneakers)). No engineering write-up from Flipkart, Myntra, Croma or any Indian comparator on competitor matching turned up. Flipkart's and Myntra's published work covers internal deduplication and visual colour variants ([Flipkart arXiv 1703.02344](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.02344)).

## The take-home design: ladder, extract, veto, rank, abstain

The pipeline below applies those findings to the stated data sources. It runs once per platform per Amazon product, and every stage is cheap enough for real time at 10–20 candidates.

**Candidate generation through a query ladder.** The platform's own search is the blocker, and recall lost here cannot be recovered later. Query in tiers, from most to least specific:

1. Brand plus model code (for example "Sony KD-55X80L" or "Nike DV2789-100").
2. Brand plus model family plus the discriminating attribute ("Samsung Galaxy S24 8GB 256GB", "Sony Bravia 55 inch X80L").
3. Brand plus family alone, which surfaces sibling variants and proves the product line exists on that platform.
4. For Myntra apparel, brand plus product type plus colour ("Puma polo t-shirt navy").

Leave colour out of phone and TV queries, because retailers keep it in the variant selector. Union at least two tiers before declaring absence. Flipkart's product URLs are a known complication. One itm ID can serve several colour slugs ([vivo Y300 a](https://www.flipkart.com/vivo-y300-5g-titanium-silver-256-gb/p/itm1f389c323bc6b), [b](https://www.flipkart.com/vivo-y300-5g-phantom-purple-256-gb/p/itm1f389c323bc6b)), and a family-level hit often lands on a sibling variant. Follow the variant selector to the exact RAM, storage and colour page before reading any stock data, and never report a sibling's stock status.

**Extract once, compare deterministically.** For the Amazon source and every candidate, produce `{brand, model_family, qualifiers[], model_code, ram_gb, storage_gb, screen_in, colour_raw, colour_base, product_type, pack_count, ean}`. Take structured fields first: Croma's Model Number, RAM, Storage, Brand Color and EAN; Flipkart's JSON-LD `brand` and `color`; Myntra's `brand` and `primaryColour`. Fill the gaps with title regexes and then the LLM. The comparison itself is plain code. This "extract-then-decide" split avoids the LLM's main failure, overweighting shared attributes, because any single conflicting field forces a reject. The table below gives the rules by category.

| Category | Hard vetoes (when both sides have the field) | Soft ranking signals | Notes on sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smartphones | Brand (with an alias map, e.g. Mi = Xiaomi); model family and qualifier set (S24 ≠ S24+ ≠ S24 FE; 4G ≠ 5G); RAM; storage; model-code base | Fuzzy title score, official colour name | Ignore virtual RAM. Croma's separate RAM and storage fields are the most reliable; Flipkart storage is in the title and RAM in highlights |
| TVs | Brand; screen size ±1"; model-code base (size digits must agree with screen_in) | Series and year, panel type | Snap cm to standard sizes; 139, 139.7 and 138.71 cm all mean 55" |
| Sneakers | Brand; style-code prefix and colour suffix when both present; primary base colour; gender | Ordered colour list overlap, image pHash/CLIP | Myntra gives only `primaryColour`, so compare the primary colour alone |
| T-shirts, polos, jerseys | Brand; product type (polo ≠ tee); base colour; pack count; jersey team, season and kit (home/away) | Fit, sleeve and collar terms; image similarity | Size is not an identity attribute; Myntra lists each colour separately |

Colour needs two modes. For phones and TVs, compare the **official marketing name** (Onyx Black, Cobalt Violet) within the same model, because each model's colour set is small and closed and retailers reuse those names. Fall back to base colour only when one side lacks the official name. For apparel and sneakers, map both sides to a base vocabulary of 15–20 colours seeded from Myntra's `primaryColour` values. Use a synonym dictionary first and the LLM for names the dictionary does not cover. Treat related families (Navy ≈ Blue) as a soft match rather than a veto. Colour in CIELAB space with ΔE thresholds, as Wayfair and Lyst use, applies only when you have RGB values, for example from images ([Wayfair](https://aboutwayfair.com/careers/tech-blog/from-rgb-to-descriptive-color-names-wayfairs-in-house-color-algorithms-to-improve-customer-shopping-experience)). A null field on one side counts as "unknown", not as a pass. Allow such a candidate through, but cap its confidence.

**Rank, accept, abstain.** Among the survivors, rank on a weighted fuzzy title score, a penalty for any unit-matched number that differs, a bonus for an exact official colour name, and an instant accept on a validated EAN match. Accept only when exactly one candidate clears the threshold with a clear margin. When two or more survive, which usually means apparel, break the tie in this order: exact colour name, then image pHash or CLIP similarity, then one listwise LLM "select" call with a forced enum output (`match / different_variant / different_product / insufficient_info`) at temperature 0. When nothing survives but the family-level query returned siblings, return "Not listed" with high confidence, because the product line is there and this variant is not. Following the arXiv 2608.25037 paper and Amazon's VARM work, distinguish an *exact match* from a *variant of the same parent* ([arXiv 2410.02779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02779)). Record the sibling as evidence, but never as the answer.

**Three-state output, plus an honest fourth.** Before any page is allowed to support "Not listed", classify the fetch as OK, EMPTY_RESULTS, BLOCKED or ERROR. Use a sentinel for each platform: Flipkart's "no results" text, or Myntra's search JSON present with an empty products array. Flipkart and Amazon.in run strong bot protection, especially during festival sales ([Actowiz guide](https://actowizsolutions.com/flipkart-amazon-meesho-myntra-data-extraction-guide.php)), so a CAPTCHA page must yield "Unknown / check failed" and never "Not listed". For a confirmed match, read stock status from the matched variant's structured data:

| Platform | Field | In stock | Out of stock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flipkart | JSON-LD `offers.availability` (schema.org ItemAvailability) | InStock, LimitedAvailability, OnlineOnly; PreOrder flagged separately | OutOfStock, SoldOut, Discontinued; also check visible "Sold Out", "Coming Soon" and "Notify Me" banners |
| Myntra | `inventoryInfo[].available` per size | Any size available | No size available |
| Croma | Apify `in_stock` | true | false; fix one pincode, since availability can vary by location |

The ItemAvailability vocabulary has 12 values ([schema.org](https://schema.org/ItemAvailability)), so map all 12 explicitly rather than testing only for "InStock". Return the label together with its evidence: matched URL, the fields that matched, the vetoed candidates and the reason for each veto, a confidence score, and the fetch status. Price2Spy found that "false positives" often turn out to be real matches the labellers missed, so every decision needs to be auditable ([Price2Spy](https://www.price2spy.com/blog/part-3-for-ml-experts-why-is-product-matching-so-difficult/)).

**Evaluate on hard negatives, by category.** Build a golden set of roughly 100–200 Amazon products across the three platforms, deliberately loaded with corner cases: the same phone in a different RAM or storage, the same TV series in a different size or year, the same shoe in a different colourway, and a single item versus a pack. Report precision and coverage separately for each category and each platform pair. Tune the accept threshold until precision reaches about 98%, following the calibrated auto-accept in arXiv 2608.25037. Then measure recall at that threshold. A golden-set size like this is a sensible starting point but has no published source. No public source gives numeric thresholds, and none measures how often each Indian retailer exposes EAN or model codes, so measure both on your own sample before fixing the rules.

## Conclusion

Product matching has changed which part of the problem is hard. LLMs have largely solved reading messy titles: they extract and normalize attributes at 90–96 F1. They have not solved deciding identity, because their characteristic error is false confidence when two listings share most of their attributes, and that is exactly the error a stock checker cannot afford. The design that follows splits the work. The LLM extracts, code decides, and the LLM is called back only as a tiebreaker on what survives. This is also the cheapest design, because every LLM call is optional, and every decision can be explained by the specific field that vetoed it.

The second implication concerns what counts as a correct answer. For this system, a well-founded "Not listed" or "check failed" is worth more than a confident guess. The four categories are not equally achievable, either. Phones and TVs reduce to a few parseable numbers and codes and should reach high precision with high coverage. Sneakers depend on whether a style code is present. Apparel coverage will stay well below electronics even at commercial scale. A take-home that reports exact matches, variant siblings, and per-category precision and coverage separately will show more about the problem than one that reports a single headline accuracy.
